# Case-analysis methods and discussion draft — 20 October 2026

Status: report-ready draft grounded in a new diagnostic replay of preserved case inputs. Numerical claims below are traceable to the [analysis tables and audit](../../results/nuscenes/bevfusion/case_analysis/20261020/README.md). Literature connections identify relevant design ideas; no mitigation has been implemented or evaluated here.

## Methods: paired diagnostic analysis

Six previously selected nuScenes validation frames were reanalysed using the preserved ground truth and predictions from the LiDAR-only and camera–LiDAR pipelines. The inputs had already undergone the saved nuScenes evaluation filters; these filters were not rerun for this analysis. The original input archive was checked against its recorded SHA-256 before processing. Matching required identical class labels and a global ground-plane centre distance strictly below 2 m. Predictions were processed in descending score order and assigned to the nearest unmatched ground-truth object, with prediction index resolving equal-score ties. The original confidence threshold was 0.30. Replaying this procedure reproduced all saved match, false-positive and false-negative lists for both models across the six frames.

The detailed analysis considers scene-0332 and scene-0562, selected from the existing illustrative cases for contrasting outcomes and class coverage. Ground-truth identities are retained within each frame so that fusion-only matches, LiDAR-only matches and shared matches can be distinguished. Thresholds of 0.10 and 0.50 were added as exploratory sensitivity checks, with the distance and class rules unchanged. For originally discordant objects, the nearest same-class candidate's score and centre error were extracted independently of eligibility at 0.30. Nearby eligible candidates and their assignments were also recorded to distinguish score rejection from assignment competition. Localisation differences were computed on shared ground-truth identities rather than comparing errors over different matched populations.

This procedure measures diagnostic operating-point behaviour. It does not replace official nuScenes AP, which summarises precision–recall behaviour rather than recall at one chosen confidence threshold. The frames were selected by outcome and subsequently reviewed visually, favouring crowded scenes and frequent classes. They cannot estimate population failure frequency. The pipelines have separate pretrained weights and training histories, so their differences are not a controlled camera ablation.

## Discussion: aggregate gains and individual regressions coexist

The positive aggregate fusion result does not imply that every object or frame improves. At confidence 0.30, scene-0332 contains 46 filtered objects and fusion matches 33, compared with 22 for LiDAR-only. Precision is 91.67% for both pipelines, while recall rises from 47.83% to 71.74%. The 11 additional matches span five cars, four pedestrians and two trucks. In contrast, scene-0562 contains 38 filtered objects and fusion matches 14 versus 21 for LiDAR-only, while false positives increase from two to three. The seven LiDAR-only matches comprise four barriers, two pedestrians and one car. These opposing examples contextualise the full-validation aggregate benefit without contradicting it: an average improvement can coexist with local regressions.

The new object-level data refine the meaning of the visual figures. All 18 discordant objects in the two frames have a nearby same-class prediction from the model labelled as missing them, with the nearest candidate scoring below 0.30. Seventeen have no eligible same-class candidate within 2 m. The remaining pedestrian has an eligible nearby candidate that is assigned to another, closer pedestrian. This identifies score eligibility and one-to-one assignment as immediate explanations of the diagnostic labels; it does not establish why the networks produce different scores. Describing the objects simply as absent detections would hide this distinction.

For example, the highlighted car in scene-0562 has a LiDAR candidate scoring 0.309195 at 0.077372 m centre error and a fusion candidate scoring 0.286567 at 0.095084 m. The original diagnostic labels these as a match and a miss because their scores straddle 0.30, despite both centres being close to the same ground truth. Conversely, the highlighted truck in scene-0332 has a lower-score LiDAR candidate with a smaller centre error than its fusion counterpart. This demonstrates that score eligibility, centre accuracy and the existence of a plausible candidate are separate properties. It does not prove score miscalibration: probability calibration requires a separate assessment on a suitable sample, and centre accuracy alone does not establish complete 3D box correctness.

## Discussion: a threshold change is a tradeoff, not a remedy

Threshold sensitivity shows why a single operating point is insufficient for diagnosis. At threshold 0.10, scene-0332 yields 37 LiDAR matches and 45 fusion matches, but also 15 and 12 false positives respectively. At 0.50, the match counts fall to 17 and 20. In scene-0562, the original seven-match gap becomes a tie at nine matches at 0.50, with zero false positives for both. Even that tie does not mean identical detections: only eight matches are shared. Lowering the threshold recovers nine of the original 11 LiDAR misses in scene-0332 and six of the seven fusion misses in scene-0562. However, the additional false positives rule out presenting threshold reduction as an established improvement without a stated application cost for misses and false alarms.

The cases motivate assessment of confidence and candidate assignment alongside geometric alignment. CLOCs is relevant to this project's KITTI branch because its authors' implementation explicitly replaces 3D-candidate confidence scores with fused scores before post-processing [1]. This provides a conceptual connection to the observed role of candidate scores, not evidence that CLOCs would correct these nuScenes cases. The project's inconsistent small-data CLOCs results must remain visible. TransFusion instead proposes soft cross-modal association to address inferior image conditions and misalignment [2]. It is a relevant architectural alternative for the literature discussion, but the present case replay neither implements it nor establishes that association errors caused the observed regressions. These are reasoned directions for mitigation, not measured remedies.

## Limits and contribution

The contribution of this analysis is a reproducible distinction between an operating-point miss and a missing nearby candidate, supported by per-object evidence and a threshold sensitivity check. It extends the earlier visual case selection with quantitative diagnostic data requested by the supervisor. It does not establish causal effects of lighting, occlusion, rain or sensor alignment. Existing synthetic corruption studies answer separate intervention questions on their frozen 600-frame subset; their results must not be used retrospectively to assign causes to these selected full-validation cases. Further analysis should preserve this separation and evaluate any proposed mitigation under a development/test protocol that does not tune on the displayed validation frames.
