# Cost and performance: draft report discussion

Draft date: 21 October 2026. Source details, assumptions, reproducible tables and limitations are in the [dated analysis](../../results/cost_performance/20261021/README.md). This passage describes an illustrative research mobile-robot scenario, not an agreed procurement design or measured deployment.

The evaluated fusion pipeline offers higher detection accuracy at additional computing cost. On the 600-frame nuScenes accuracy subset, fusion increases mAP from 64.3076 to 68.1318 and NDS from 69.4430 to 71.2913, with metrics displayed multiplied by 100. A separately scoped single-T4 study over 150 frames and three sessions per model measures median latency of 287.8116 ms for LiDAR-only and 544.9786 ms for fusion, and maximum peak allocated PyTorch memory of 609.8184 and 2,231.7979 MiB. Thus the accuracy advantage accompanies 1.8935 times the latency and 3.6598 times the allocated memory. These measurements do not establish energy consumption or embedded inference performance, and the accuracy and timing sample sets/settings must remain explicit.

The financial analysis considers purchased components for an illustrative mobile robot. Public listings inspected on 21 October 2026 give US$269 for a Luxonis OAK-1 and US$379 for the distinct OAK-1 PoE option, while a retailer reference listing gives US$899 for a backordered Livox Mid-360. Under quantity-one linear pricing, one camera plus one LiDAR therefore has a sensor-device subtotal of US$1,168 or US$1,278; two cameras plus one LiDAR cost US$1,437 or US$1,657; and six cameras plus one LiDAR cost US$2,513 or US$3,173. Three identical LiDARs cost US$2,697 under that reference assumption. These are dated component scenarios, not comparable quotations for the dataset equipment. Host compute, calibration, mounting, synchronisation, power integration, taxes and delivery remain unpriced additions. Separate 0%, 15% and 30% contingencies and a ±25% LiDAR-price stress test expose assumption sensitivity without presenting the resulting intervals as supplier offers.

The measured detector results cannot be attached to these hardware subtotals: the products, scanning geometry, optics and execution platforms differ from the recorded KITTI/nuScenes systems. Likewise, masking four views of an existing six-camera model cannot establish the financial or computational performance of a purpose-trained two-camera design. A one-camera-plus-one-LiDAR comparison already exists in the KITTI CLOCs work, but its gains vary across seeds and difficulty levels; it cannot support a general claim of fusion superiority. The appropriate design conclusion is conditional: select sensing and compute against stated coverage, latency, accuracy and environmental requirements, then validate the resulting pipeline and price its actual integration. Within the two measured LiDAR/fusion operating points, neither dominates on NDS and latency, but a global or monetary Pareto frontier cannot be inferred.

Its caption must state USD, access date, selected product variants, omitted costs and that no hardware-specific accuracy was measured. Detailed source records, assumptions and all sensitivity rows remain supporting material. No extra figure is required.
